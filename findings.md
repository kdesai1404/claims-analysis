\# Findings



\## Data

\- CMS DE-SynPUF Sample 1 (synthetic Medicare claims, 2008-2010)

\- 857,563 raw claims; 846,520 after merging 11,043 claim segments (total paid $863.8M, unchanged)



\## Spend concentration

\- Inpatient claims are 7.8% of claims but about 74% of spend

\- Top 10% of patients drive 51.1% of spend; top 30% drive 82.8%



\## Cost drivers

\- Average 2009 spend per patient rises with every added chronic condition: $166 (0 conditions) to $15,682 (8 conditions)



\## Data quality

\- 32,265 zero-paid claims; 5,715 missing diagnoses



\## Leakage checks

\- Potential duplicate payments: 114 groups, about $20.5K (0.002% of spend, immaterial)

\- Provider outliers (z >= 3 vs peer average for same diagnosis and claim type): 154 of 8,485 provider-diagnosis pairs, estimated review opportunity about $1.0M (0.12% of spend, upper bound)



\## Conclusion

\- Spend is driven by high-need patients, not billing anomalies. Recommend care management for patients with 5+ chronic conditions.



\## Caveats

\- Data is synthetic and from 2008-2010; results show what the method would flag, not real overbilling

\- Peer comparison does not control for patient severity

\- Provider outlier amounts are an upper bound
